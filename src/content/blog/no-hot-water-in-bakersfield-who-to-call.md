No hot water in Bakersfield almost always traces back to one of three places: the breaker panel, the pilot and thermocouple assembly on a gas tank, or the digital display on a tankless unit. ## What should I check before I call anyone?

Before calling a plumber, check three things: the breaker labeled for the water heater in your electrical panel, the pilot light viewing window on a gas tank unit, and the error code on a tankless unit's display. These three checks take under five minutes and tell you whether the problem is electrical, a dead pilot, or a tankless fault.

- **Breaker:** flip it fully off, then firmly back on. If it trips again immediately, stop, don't reset it a second time.
- **Pilot light:** look through the small window near the bottom of a gas tank. If it's out and you don't smell gas, it may just need relighting per the label on the unit.
- **Tankless display:** write down the exact code shown. Rinnai, Navien, and Rheem each use different numbering, and that code tells a plumber what part to bring.
- **Thermostat:** confirm the dial wasn't bumped to vacation mode or turned down, especially if someone else uses the garage.

## Do I smell gas near the water heater?

A gas smell near the water heater is a safety emergency, not a repair call. Leave the house immediately, don't flip switches or light anything, and call 911 or the gas utility's 24-hour emergency line from outside. In the Bakersfield area that's SoCalGas for natural gas service and PG&E for electric. Only after the utility confirms the scene is safe should you call a plumber to repair the gas line or replace a failed valve. We've written more on this in our [gas leak safety guide](/blog/gas-leak-what-to-do/), and it's worth reading once before you ever need it.

## Why did my gas water heater lose hot water?

Most gas water heaters that stop producing hot water have a pilot light that went out, a failed thermocouple that won't let gas flow to the burner, or a stuck gas control valve. A thermocouple is an inexpensive part a plumber can usually swap in under an hour. A failed gas control valve means replacing the whole valve assembly, and on an older tank that's often the point where [water heater installation](/services/water-heater-installation/) starts making more sense than another repair. If your pilot relights but won't stay lit, that's a classic thermocouple symptom, and it's a [water heater repair](/services/water-heater-repair/) call, not a DIY fix with the gas line open.

## Why did my electric water heater stop heating?

Electric water heaters usually stop producing hot water because of a tripped double-pole breaker, a blown high-limit switch, or a burned-out heating element. If the breaker trips again right after you reset it, stop resetting it. A breaker that won't hold is a sign of a short or a failed element drawing too much current, and it needs a plumber or electrician to test it with a multimeter before anything gets flipped again. Pre-1970 homes in Oleander, Westchester, and East Bakersfield often still carry original wiring runs to older tanks, and that combination is worth having a tech look at directly.

## What about tankless error codes, and is this a same-day fix?

A tankless water heater's error code almost always points to scale buildup on the heat exchanger or an ignition fault, both common in Bakersfield's hard municipal water, and both are usually same-day repairs. Manufacturers and the U.S. Department of Energy both recommend regular descaling flushes for tankless units in hard-water regions as part of routine [home equipment upkeep](https://www.energy.gov/save/home-upgrades), and skipping that service is the most common reason a tankless unit in this valley starts throwing codes years ahead of schedule. A same-day visit usually includes a multimeter test on elements and thermostats or a thermocouple test on a gas unit, reading and clearing the tankless fault code, and a written quote before any part gets replaced.
