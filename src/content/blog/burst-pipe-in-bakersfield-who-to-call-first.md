If water is shooting from a wall, ceiling, or under a slab in your Bakersfield home right now, stop and find your main shutoff valve before you do anything else. On most Bakersfield homes that means the valve near the street-side water meter or the one where the supply line enters the garage or a utility closet. Turn it clockwise until it stops. Once the water is off, come back and work through the rest of this.

## What do you do in the first 60 seconds?

Shut off the water at the main valve, then cut power to any outlet or fixture the water is touching. Do this before you grab a towel.

If the water is pooling near an outlet, a light fixture, or the electrical panel, do not walk through it to reach a switch. Flip the breaker for that circuit from a dry spot, or shut off the whole panel if you are unsure which circuit is affected. If the leak is near the water heater, shut off its dedicated valve too, a water heater keeps feeding a leak even after the main is closed if that valve stays open.

Once the water is off, move anything you can lift away from the wet area: rugs, furniture legs, electronics, boxes stored on the floor. Speed matters more than thoroughness here.

## What happens in the first 24 hours after a burst pipe?

In the first 24 hours, a plumber isolates and repairs the break while a restoration crew extracts water and sets drying equipment following the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) for water damage restoration.

Water from a burst supply line starts out as Category 1, clean water. But it does not stay clean. If it sits for more than a day, soaks into carpet padding, or picks up contaminants from building materials, it degrades toward Category 2, which means more materials need to come out rather than just dry in place. That is the practical reason restoration needs to start within hours, not days: the longer the delay, the more gets removed instead of saved.

By the end of day one, you should have a signed repair ticket from the plumber, moisture readings from the restoration crew, and drying equipment running in the affected rooms. Expect the equipment to stay in place for three to five days depending on how much material got wet.

## Will homeowners insurance pay for burst pipe damage?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe, but they generally exclude damage from a leak that was gradual or went unaddressed for a long time.

The distinction insurers draw is speed and cause: a copper line that splits from a hard freeze or a sudden pressure spike is a covered loss in most policies, while a slow pinhole leak behind a wall that soaked framing for months is often treated as a maintenance issue instead. Document everything with photos before cleanup starts, and keep every receipt, insurers expect homeowners to mitigate further damage once the leak is found, and a documented, prompt response helps the claim rather than hurting it. For a deeper look at how coverage decisions get made on a related failure type, see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Slab Leak Repair?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-slab-leak-repair/). Coverage specifics vary by insurer and policy, so [check your policy's water damage section](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) or call your agent before assuming either way.
