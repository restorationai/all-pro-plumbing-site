## How much does it cost to clear a single clogged sink, tub, or toilet?

A single fixture clog, a slow kitchen sink, a backed-up tub, or a clogged toilet, typically costs $100 to $300 to clear with a hand auger or a small powered snake. Most of that cost is the service call and 30 to 60 minutes of labor, not parts.

Toilets usually land at the lower end of that range unless the clog is caused by something lodged past the trap, which can require pulling the toilet. Kitchen sinks with grease buildup sometimes run higher because grease has to be mechanically broken up, not just pushed through. For a full fixture-by-fixture breakdown, see our [guide to what a plumber charges to unclog a drain](/blog/how-much-does-a-plumber-cost-to-unclog-a-drain/).

If a cheap chemical drain cleaner hasn't worked after one try, stop using it. Repeated pours can damage older galvanized or cast-iron pipe and make the eventual professional cleaning harder, which our [DIY versus pro drain cleaning guide](/blog/drain-cleaning-diy-vs-pro/) covers in detail.

## How much does main line drain cleaning cost?

Main line cleaning, clearing the line from your house to the city sewer or septic connection, typically runs $250 to $600 with a cable machine, and $400 to $900 when a hydro jetter is needed instead. The gap comes down to what's actually in the pipe.

Bakersfield's mature tree canopy in Downtown, Oleander/Sunset, and East Bakersfield means a lot of pre-1970 homes are still running cast-iron or clay laterals that roots have found through a cracked joint. A cable machine can punch a hole through root intrusion and restore flow for a while. It doesn't clear the pipe wall the way a jetter does, so roots tend to come back faster. If your main line clogs more than once a year, that's usually the sign a cable alone isn't solving the real problem.

If a camera inspection turns up a collapsed section, a major offset, or a belly that's holding standing water, that's no longer a cleaning job. At that point you're looking at a repair or a trenchless fix, which is a different scope than drain cleaning; see our [sewer line repair service page](/services/sewer-line-repair/) for what that process looks like.

## Hydro jetting vs snaking: which one do you actually need?

Snaking is cheaper and faster for a straightforward clog; jetting costs more but actually scours the pipe wall clean, which matters for root intrusion and grease buildup that keep coming back. Snaking punches a path through the blockage. Jetting uses high-pressure water to strip the inside of the pipe back to bare material.

For a cast-iron or clay lateral in an older Bakersfield neighborhood with a history of root problems, jetting is usually the better long-term value even though it costs more upfront, because it buys more time before the next call. For the full comparison of when each method applies, read [hydro jetting vs snaking](/blog/hydro-jetting-vs-snaking/).

## Is a camera inspection worth paying for?

A camera inspection, typically $125 to $300 as an add-on to a cleaning call, is worth it any time a main line clog has come back more than once or the home is old enough to carry cast-iron or clay drains. It shows exactly what's wrong instead of guessing.

Without a camera, a technician is cleaning blind: they know the line is flowing again, not why it clogged or whether it will clog again in six weeks. With a camera, you see the actual root ball, the actual offset joint, or the actual belly holding water, and you get a real answer about whether cleaning solves the problem or just delays a repair. Many companies, including ours, apply the inspection fee toward the cost of a repair if one is found and scheduled.

## Why does the cheapest drain cleaning quote sometimes cost more in the end?

A rock-bottom flat-rate quote often means a short cable run with no root-cutting head and no camera time, which clears the symptom but not the cause in a root-packed cast-iron line. You pay less today and pay again in three months.

Watch for quotes that don't specify the method (cable vs jetter), don't mention camera verification on a repeat clog, or quote a flat price before anyone has actually looked at the line. A written quote should name the method, state whether a camera inspection is included or optional, and spell out the after-hours rate if it applies.
