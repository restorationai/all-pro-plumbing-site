**TL;DR:** A plumber charges roughly $95–$250 to snake a single fixture drain, $200–$400 for a branch line clog, and $300–$600 or more to clear a main sewer line. Camera inspection adds $150–$350. Hydro jetting runs $350–$850 depending on line length and buildup. What you actually pay depends on which drain is clogged, how deep the blockage sits, and whether the clog is grease, roots, or something structural. This breakdown covers each scenario so you can budget accurately before anyone shows up at your door.

Your kitchen sink is draining in slow motion, or your tub backed up into your shower, or, worst case, multiple fixtures are gurgling at once. The price you'll pay to fix it depends almost entirely on *which* drain is blocked and *what's* blocking it. A $150 snake job and a $600 hydro-jet call are both legitimate drain cleaning; they're just solving different problems. Here's how to read which situation you're in.

## How much does it cost to snake a single fixture drain?

Snaking a single fixture drain, a kitchen sink, bathroom sink, tub, or toilet, typically costs $95–$250 in the Bakersfield area. A plumber runs a drum auger or hand cable through the drain to break up or retrieve the clog, which is usually hair, soap scum, grease, or a small foreign object. The job takes 30–60 minutes in most cases.

Single-fixture clogs are the most common call and the most straightforward to price. The range reflects travel time, drain access (a floor cleanout is easier than pulling a P-trap under a pedestal sink), and whether the first cable pass clears it or the tech needs to switch to a longer or heavier cable. If you've already tried a plunger and a hand auger with no luck, a professional snake is the right next step, not a chemical drain cleaner, which can damage older pipes and rarely clears anything beyond a surface-level clog. For a deeper look at why chemicals fall short, see our post [Chemical Drain Cleaners vs Calling a Plumber: What Actually Works](/blog/drain-cleaning-diy-vs-pro/).

## How much does it cost to clear a branch line or shared drain?

A branch line serves a group of fixtures, say, the hall bathroom's sink, tub, and toilet all sharing one 3-inch line before it connects to the main. Clearing a branch line typically runs $200–$400.

Branch line clogs are harder to reach than fixture-level blockages. The tech usually works from a cleanout fitting in the wall or floor rather than from the fixture itself, and the cable needs to travel farther. In pre-1970 homes in Oleander, Westchester, and East Bakersfield, where original cast-iron drains are still common, branch lines sometimes have corroded sections or offset joints that catch debris and require more passes. If the tech can't clear it with a cable, that's when a camera goes in.

## How much does it cost to clear a main sewer line?

Main sewer line clearing costs $300–$600 for a standard cable job, and $350–$850 for hydro jetting, depending on line length and what's in it. The main line is the 4-inch pipe that carries everything from your house to the city sewer at the street, usually 50–100 feet or more.

If multiple drains in your home are slow or backing up at the same time, the clog is almost certainly in the main. This is the most expensive drain call because the line is longer, the blockage is often denser (compacted grease, roots, or both), and access usually means pulling a cleanout cap in the yard or working from a roof vent stack. Bakersfield's mature tree canopy, especially in older neighborhoods near downtown, Oleander, and East Bakersfield, means root intrusion is a real and recurring cause of main-line backups. Tree roots find hairline cracks in older clay or cast-iron laterals and grow in over years. A cable can cut through roots and restore flow, but it doesn't seal the crack that let them in.

## What does a camera inspection cost, and when do you need one?

A sewer camera inspection costs $150–$350 as a standalone service, though many plumbers apply part of that fee toward any repair work that follows. A waterproof camera is fed through the cleanout and transmits live video of the pipe interior.

You need a camera when: (1) a main-line clog comes back within weeks or months of being cleared, (2) the cable hits an obstruction it can't break through, or (3) you're buying a home and want to know the condition of the lateral before closing. Camera footage shows whether you're dealing with a grease buildup that hydro jetting can fix, active root intrusion that will keep coming back, a cracked or offset pipe section, or a belly (a low sag in the line where solids settle). That distinction matters because it's the difference between a $400 cleaning and a [sewer line repair](/services/sewer-line-repair/) conversation.

## How much does hydro jetting cost?

Hydro jetting costs $350–$850 for a residential main line in Bakersfield, with the range driven by line length, access, and how much buildup is present. A hydro jet uses high-pressure water, typically 3,000–4,000 PSI, to scour the pipe walls clean rather than just punching a hole through the clog.

Snaking clears a path; hydro jetting clears the pipe. For grease-heavy lines (restaurants, households that cook a lot), root-infested laterals, or any line where the same clog has come back more than once, jetting is the more durable fix. It's also the method that makes the most sense before a camera inspection, since a clean pipe gives a clearer picture. For a full comparison of when each method is the right call, see our post [Hydro Jetting vs Snaking: Which Drain Cleaning Method Do You Need?](/blog/hydro-jetting-vs-snaking/)

## What drives the final price up (or down)?

Several factors push a drain cleaning bill above or below the ranges above:

- **Access**: A cleanout fitting at grade level is easy. A cleanout buried under a concrete slab, behind drywall, or only accessible through a roof vent takes more time.
- **Pipe material and age**: Cast-iron and clay pipes common in Bakersfield's pre-1970 housing stock are more fragile than PVC. A tech will use a lighter cable or lower jetting pressure to avoid damaging them, which can mean more passes.
- **Clog composition**: Grease clears faster than compacted rags or root masses. Roots that have been growing for years may require multiple cable passes before jetting is even possible.
- **Time of day**: Standard business-hours pricing applies during the day. After-hours or weekend dispatch typically adds a service call premium, ask about this when you call.
- **Bundled services**: If a camera inspection leads directly to a repair, many plumbers credit the inspection fee toward the repair quote. Ask before you authorize the camera.

## When does a drain clog become a sewer repair conversation?

A drain clog becomes a sewer repair conversation when the same main-line blockage recurs within 6–12 months, or when a camera reveals structural damage, cracks, root intrusion through the pipe wall, offset joints, or a collapsed section. At that point, clearing the line again buys time but doesn't fix the underlying problem.

For Bakersfield homeowners in older neighborhoods, this is a real inflection point. The mature trees along streets in Oleander, Westchester, and East Bakersfield are beautiful, but their root systems are relentless. If a camera shows roots entering through a crack in a clay lateral, the repair options are pipe lining (a cured-in-place liner that seals the crack from inside) or pipe bursting (replacing the old pipe by pulling a new one through it).
