## What should a proper AC diagnosis include?

A proper AC diagnosis checks refrigerant pressure, electrical components, and airflow, not just one of the three. A technician who replaces a part without checking why it failed is guessing, not diagnosing.

That means connecting manifold gauges to the service ports and reading pressures against the system's rated refrigerant, testing the capacitor and contactor with a multimeter, checking the condenser fan motor's amp draw, and inspecting airflow at the return and supply side along with the filter. A capacitor that fails every year, for example, is often a symptom of a compressor pulling extra amps, not a standalone part failure. If your AC is running but the house still isn't cooling, [Air Conditioner Not Cooling](/blog/air-conditioner-not-cooling/) walks through the troubleshooting order a technician should actually follow. Ask upfront whether the diagnostic fee rolls into the repair cost if you proceed; most Bakersfield companies, including All Pro, credit it toward the job.

## Repair or replace: what do age and refrigerant type tell you?

A central AC system older than 12 to 15 years running on R-22 refrigerant is usually a replacement, not a repair, because R-22 production in the US ended in 2020 and remaining stock is expensive. Plenty of pre-1990s homes near Oleander/Sunset and Westchester still run original R-22 condensers that have spent three decades baking through Bakersfield's triple-digit summers.

If the data plate on your outdoor unit lists R-22 and the technician finds a refrigerant leak, recovering and recharging that refrigerant can cost more than the compressor underneath it is worth. Refrigerant recovery itself is federally regulated: only an [EPA Section 608](https://www.epa.gov/section608) certified technician can legally handle it, which is one more reason to confirm who you're hiring. Systems under 8 to 10 years old running R-410A are usually worth repairing unless the compressor itself has failed. A rule many technicians use: if a single repair runs more than 30 to 40 percent of what a new system costs installed, replacement is the better math. ## Which California license should you check before the tech comes out?

Any AC repair company working in California should hold an active CSLB contractor license, and you can verify it in under two minutes using the state's [license lookup tool](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx). An active license also typically carries bonding and insurance requirements that protect you if something goes wrong mid-repair.

All Pro's California contractor license number is 960566. Run that number, or any other company's, through the CSLB tool before scheduling. A license that's expired, suspended, or doesn't exist is the single clearest red flag in this whole process, and it takes less time to check than it does to read this paragraph.

## What should you ask about pricing before the tech comes?

Ask whether the diagnostic fee is flat and whether it's credited toward the repair, and ask for a written quote before any work begins. A company that won't commit to either answer on the phone usually won't commit to one in person either.

Specifically: is the repair quote flat-rate per job or billed hourly? Does it list parts and labor separately, so you can see what you're actually paying for? And is there an after-hours or weekend surcharge? All Pro runs 24/7, so ask directly whether a late-night call carries a different rate than a weekday appointment, same as you'd ask any company whose hours extend past normal business hours.

## When does a ductless mini-split make more sense than repeated central AC repairs?

A ductless mini-split makes more sense than another central AC repair when the ductwork itself is the real problem, when you're cooling a single added room, or when the system needs full replacement anyway and you want zone control. Converted garages, sunrooms, and additions are common around Bakersfield, and extending existing ductwork into them is often more expensive than a dedicated [ductless mini-split](/services/ductless-mini-splits/).

Leaky attic ductwork is a recurring issue in older Bakersfield housing stock, and a mini-split sidesteps that loss entirely for the room it serves. Newer builds out in Riverlakes Ranch rarely need this kind of patch, but if you're weighing a full [AC installation](/services/ac-installation/) anyway, it's worth asking your technician whether a mini-split or a multi-zone system fits the house better than a straight central-air swap.
