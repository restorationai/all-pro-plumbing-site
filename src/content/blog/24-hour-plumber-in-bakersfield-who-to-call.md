## What actually counts as a plumbing emergency?

A plumbing emergency is any failure that's actively damaging your home, risking your family's safety, or cutting off a basic utility with no safe workaround. That includes a burst or actively leaking supply pipe, a sewage backup coming up through a drain or toilet, a gas odor near a water heater or furnace, and a total loss of water or hot water in a home with infants, elderly residents, or anyone on home medical equipment.

A slow drain, a running toilet, or a dripping outdoor spigot is not an emergency, even if it's annoying at 10 PM. Those can wait for a standard weekday appointment and save you the after-hours trip charge, which typically runs $75 to $200 above daytime rates in the Bakersfield market depending on the hour.

If you smell gas anywhere near an appliance, treat that differently than a water emergency. Leave the house immediately, don't flip light switches or use your phone inside, and call 911 or PG&E's 24-hour emergency line from outside. Only after the utility has confirmed the scene is safe should you call a plumber for the repair or pressure test. See our [gas leak safety guide](/blog/gas-leak-what-to-do/) for the full sequence.

## How do you find a real 24 hour plumber in Bakersfield?

A genuine 24 hour plumber has a live person or dispatcher answering the phone after hours, not a voicemail box, and can tell you a realistic arrival window on that first call. If you get a recording asking you to leave a message and wait for a callback, that company is not actually running 24/7 dispatch no matter what their website says.

Before you commit, run through this quick checklist:

1. **Ask who answers at night.** A dispatcher who can quote a rough arrival time (not "someone will call you back") is doing real after-hours scheduling.
2. **Verify the license on the spot.** Every plumbing contractor working in California needs an active license through the [Contractors State License Board](https://www.cslb.ca.gov). You can look up any license number on their site in under two minutes while you're still on hold.
3. **Get the after-hours rate before the truck leaves the yard.** A legitimate company quotes the trip fee and hourly or flat rate over the phone, not after the technician is already standing in your kitchen.
4. **Ask what's included in that rate.** Diagnosis only, or diagnosis plus a standard repair? Get it in writing, even a text, before work starts.

## What should you do while you're waiting for the plumber?

The single most useful thing you can do before anyone arrives is stop the water at the source. Find your home's main shutoff, usually a lever or wheel valve where the supply line enters near the street-facing side of the house or next to the water heater, and turn it fully off.

If the leak is isolated to one fixture, like a supply line under a sink or behind a toilet, you can often close just that fixture's angle stop instead of killing water to the whole house. Mop or towel off standing water on hard flooring to limit staining, and move anything valuable off the floor nearby. Don't attempt to cut into a wall or slab yourself to "get a look" at the pipe. That almost always turns a contained leak into a bigger repair.

## What happens after the emergency call, and how do you avoid overpaying?

A properly run emergency call ends with a written diagnosis and a clear repair quote before any permanent work starts, not a surprise invoice. The technician should explain what failed, why, and what the fix costs, with the after-hours portion broken out separately from the repair itself.
