## Why Bodfish Properties See AC Installation and Replacement Needs

Bodfish sits at elevation in the Kern River Valley, which means intense daytime sun, dry heat, and wide day-to-night temperature swings that put more cycling stress on a compressor than a milder coastal climate would. A lot of the housing stock out here is older manufactured homes and site-built cabins on acreage, many still running R-22 systems or units sized decades ago for a smaller, shadier footprint before additions or sunroom conversions were added. Because the area isn't on a municipal natural gas line in most pockets, a good number of homes pair the AC with a propane furnace or heat pump setup, which changes how we size and connect the new condenser. Dust and fine grit blowing off the hills around the lake also load up condenser coils faster than in a typical suburban install, which shortens the usable life of a unit that isn't maintained on a tighter schedule.

## Our AC Installation and Replacement Process in Bodfish

We start with a load calculation that accounts for Bodfish's sun exposure and elevation rather than defaulting to a square-footage rule of thumb, because an undersized or oversized unit both cause problems here: one runs constantly and still underperforms, the other short-cycles and leaves the home humid and uneven. From there we check existing ductwork, which on older Kern River Valley homes is often undersized or leaking at the joints, and we address that before connecting a new high-SEER2 system so the equipment isn't working against bad distribution. Permitting runs through Kern County, and we handle that paperwork as part of the install. Pad and line-set placement gets extra attention on properties with dirt or gravel driveways and seasonal runoff, since a condenser set too low can end up sitting in standing water after a hard winter rain. Once the system is set, we commission it with a full startup check, refrigerant charge verification, and airflow test before we call the job done.
